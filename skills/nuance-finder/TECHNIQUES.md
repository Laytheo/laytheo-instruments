# Persuasion and manipulation techniques

A working catalog for naming, precisely, what a piece of content is doing. Use it as a lens,
not a checklist: name the specific techniques in play and quote the exact phrases that
execute them, rather than gesturing at "bias." Most content uses two or three of these, not
twelve. And apply the catalog symmetrically: a counter-narrative or a "respectable" source
can run the same plays as the viral post.

Naming a technique is a description of *form*, not a verdict on *truth*. A claim can use
loaded language and still be substantially accurate; a claim can be perfectly neutral in
tone and still be false. Keep the two separate.

## Framing and language

- **Loaded / emotive language** — word choices that smuggle in a judgment. "Slammed,"
  "destroyed," "biohazard," "regime," "thugs." Look at what a neutral synonym would do to the
  sentence.
- **Euphemism / dysphemism** — softening or hardening the same act by name ("enhanced
  interrogation" vs "torture"; "rioters" vs "protesters").
- **Weasel words** — vague attribution that dodges accountability. "Some say," "people are
  asking," "experts warn," "reportedly," with no named source.
- **False novelty** — presenting a routine or long-standing thing as unprecedented. "For the
  first time ever," "no one is talking about." Usually false and easy to check.
- **Loaded question / presupposition** — a framing that embeds a contested claim as a given
  ("why is X still refusing to...?" assumes the refusal).

## Evidence and numbers

- **Missing denominator / base rate** — a raw count with no context that would size it ("500
  cases" out of how many, over what period, compared to what baseline).
- **Cherry-picking** — a real but unrepresentative subset of the evidence, with the rest
  omitted (one cold day to dispute a warming trend; one study against the body of research).
- **Misleading statistic** — a true number framed to mislead: rate vs absolute count,
  percentage of a tiny base, a correct figure attached to the wrong cause.
- **False precision** — oddly exact numbers used to manufacture authority ("2,200 pounds")
  when the underlying figure is an estimate or a sum of unlike things.
- **Decontextualized quote** — a real quotation stripped of the surrounding words that change
  its meaning.
- **Decontextualized, recycled, or staged image** — a real image from a different event,
  place, or year, or one cropped, mislabeled, or staged to imply something it does not show.
- **Fabricated detail** — a specific, vivid, invented particular ("treating US food like a
  biohazard") grafted onto a real story to make it feel concrete.

## Logic and causation

- **False cause** — treating sequence or correlation as causation ("X happened, then Y, so X
  caused Y").
- **False dilemma** — presenting two options as the only ones when more exist.
- **Slippery slope** — asserting that one step inevitably leads to an extreme end, with the
  intermediate steps unargued.
- **Strawman** — restating the other side's position in a weaker or distorted form, then
  defeating that.
- **Whataboutism** — deflecting a claim by pointing at an unrelated fault elsewhere instead
  of addressing it.
- **Moving the goalposts** — changing the standard of proof once the original one is met.

## Social proof and authority

- **Manufactured consensus** — implying that "everyone," "the science," or "we all" agree,
  to make dissent feel deviant.
- **Appeal to dubious or vague authority** — citing an unnamed, unqualified, or
  irrelevant "expert" or institution.
- **Astroturfing** — coordinated activity dressed up as spontaneous grassroots reaction
  (suspiciously uniform language, timing, or accounts).

## Emotion and identity

- **Outrage / engagement bait** — content engineered to provoke a strong share-now reaction;
  the emotion is the product, the information is the bait.
- **Fear appeal** — leaning on threat and safety to bypass scrutiny ("they are coming for
  your...").
- **Us-versus-them / identity framing** — sorting a story into a tribal conflict so the
  reader responds as a team member rather than an evaluator.
- **Nut-picking** — holding up one extreme or fringe example as representative of a whole
  group.
- **Guilt by association** — discrediting a claim or person by linking them to something
  disreputable, rather than engaging the substance.

## Source and provenance

- **Satire presented as real** — content that originated as satire or parody, circulated
  stripped of that context.
- **Old story recycled as new** — a genuine past event recirculated as if current, often
  during a related news cycle.
- **AI-generated or manipulated media** — synthetic images, audio, or video, or selectively
  edited real footage, presented as authentic.
- **Anonymous or unverifiable sourcing** — the whole claim rests on a screenshot, a "leak,"
  or an account that cannot be traced or checked.
- **Headline / body mismatch** — a headline (or caption) that the underlying article or
  source does not actually support; many sharers never read past it.
